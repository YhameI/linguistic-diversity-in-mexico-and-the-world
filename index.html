Mexico

1. DAY OF THE DEAD (November 1st and 2nd)

 This is one of the most important Mexican traditions. Families remember their loved ones who have passed away. They create colorful altars with photographs, candles, marigold flowers, food, and other special objects. People also visit cemeteries to honor their families.

2. INDEPENDENCE DAY (September 16th)

 This celebration commemorates Mexico's independence from Spain in 1810. People decorate streets and houses with Mexican flags. There are parades, traditional music, fireworks, and celebrations in town squares.

3. GUELAGUETZA (July)

 This traditional festival takes place in Oaxaca. People from different communities gather to celebrate their culture. They wear traditional costumes, perform regional dances, play music, and share typical food from their regions.

Australia

1. AUSTRALIA DAY (January 26th)

 Every year the country holds special events to honour their nationality. The 26th is a date of significance as it was Jan 26th 1788 that the British first landed in Australia.

Local community barbeques are a favourite way to enjoy the festivities and a wonderful way to get to know friends and neighbours. Outdoor concerts are also popular, showcasing Australian musical talent. Festivals and fireworks are also common and take place over the long weekend

This week-long tradition has been a fixture of the Australian calendar for some time, and is a time of remembrance, mourning, and observance for the Native lives that were lost as a result of colonisation.

The week spans from the first Sunday in July to the following Sunday every year, and has been a week-long event since 1975.

2. SYDNEY FESTIVAL (January)

 Is a three-week-long arts and culture festival held every January, that celebrates the exciting culture that Sydney has to offer.

It is one of the largest arts festivals in the entire world with around 500,000 people attending each year.

The festival has loads of free and paid events, but there is something to suit everyone from visual art, comedy performances, and the Sydney symphony under the stars!

3. ANZAC DAY (April 25th)

 Is a point of remembrance for all fallen soldiers who have fought and died for the freedoms of Australia and New Zealand. The day is recognised in many countries across the world including the UK, Poland, South Sudan, India, and Cyprus. With many commemorations taking place at Australian war memories the world over.

Canada

1. THANKSGIVING (October)

 In Canada, Thanksgiving is a holiday rooted in European English culture. It celebrates the harvest season and gives thanks for the bounty of the land.
According to the Canadian Encyclopedia, the first Thanksgiving celebrated in Canada took place on November 6, 1879, although Indigenous peoples already had a long tradition of celebrating the fall harvest.
The Smithsonian Institution states that some First Nations sought ways to ensure a good harvest through dances and rituals.
There is, however, a dispute—acknowledged by the Canadian Encyclopedia itself—regarding that first celebration. Some have argued that the ceremony held by Sir Martin Frobisher, an English sailor who led expeditions to the Arctic, was not actually a Thanksgiving celebration. The dispute stems mainly from the reason for giving thanks—a question that persists to this day—since gratitude is not limited to the harvest. In the modern world, giving thanks is more closely linked to general well-being and the good things around us.
Although it began as a holiday to give thanks for the harvest, today Thanksgiving is a civic holiday and, above all, a family holiday.
Prior to 1879, French settlers also observed the holiday, but it wasn’t until 1957 that Thanksgiving was established as an annual event celebrated on the second Monday in October. Important fact: It is an official holiday in all Canadian provinces and territories except Prince Edward Island, New Brunswick, and Nova Scotia.
Religion played a central role in the celebration, but over the years, its religious character gradually took a back seat, giving way to a civic and, above all, family-oriented holiday.

Although it began as a celebration to give thanks for the harvest, today Thanksgiving is a civic and, above all, family-oriented holiday.

2. CANADA DAY (July 1st)

 Canada Day, is one of Canada's most cherished traditions, the country's national holiday. Every year on July 1st, Canadians fill the streets, taking advantage of the warmer weather, to commemorate Canada's independence from Great Britain in 1867. Since then, Canada has been a sovereign nation, having been established through the signing of the British North America Act, which created four separate territories: Upper Canada (present-day Ontario), Lower Canada (present-day Quebec), Nova Scotia, and New Brunswick. The national holiday was formally established in 1867 and was originally called Dominion Day. In 1982, it was renamed "Canada Day." The most significant events of this celebration are usually parades, outdoor parties, and fireworks displays. While no city is left out of this celebration, Canada's capital city, Ottawa, is known for its vibrant festivities. She hosts the official ceremony and celebrations in the gardens of the national parliament buildings. Montreal and Toronto also boast some of the most significant parades in the country. As expected, all the celebrations have a strong patriotic character. The streets are filled with Canadian flags, and many people paint their faces red and white, the national colors.

3. REMEMBRANCE DAY (November 11th)

 Remembrance Day is commemorated every year on November 11 to honor Canadians who served and died in the armed forces. There are many ways to observe Remembrance Day in Ontario and to learn more about Canada’s military history. From November 5 to 11, Veterans Week is observed, a time to recognize and reflect on the service of Canadian veterans. On November 8, National Indigenous Veterans Day recognizes the contributions of Indigenous peoples to military service. On November 11, Canada commemorates Remembrance Day to honor those who served in times of war, conflict, and peace around the world and to remember those who have fallen. November 11 is significant because World War I ended with the signing of an armistice at 11 a.m. on the 11th day of the 11th month of 1918. In 1931, Armistice Day became Remembrance Day in Canada and other Commonwealth countries.

Spain

1. LAS FALLAS (March)

 Continuing with one of Spain's best-known festivals beyond its borders: Las Fallas in Valencia. Celebrated in the city of Valencia during March, Las Fallas are known for their huge papier-mâché and wooden sculptures called “ninots”.

During the five-day festival, the city of Valencia is filled with colorful parades, music, and fireworks. The culmination of the Fallas is the “cremà”, where the sculptures are burned in a spectacular display of flames. Although only one of all the “ninots” manages to be saved from the flames, by popular vote, and goes on display in the Museo Local de Fallas (local museum) with the “pardoned ninots” from previous years. If you like fireworks and gunpowder, these are your parties.

2. LOS SANFERMINES (July)

 Immortalized by Ernest Hemingway in his work "Fiesta", the San Fermin Day attracts brave runners and many spectators. The Sanfermines begin with the “chupinazo” and are always held on July 6, one of the most famous fiestas in Spain and abroad.

During nine days, the streets of Pamplona are filled with running of the bulls through the narrow streets of the city. Enjoy days of music, dances, and activities for all ages.

3. LA TOMATINA DE BUÑOL (August)

 What happens when you mix a small town with tons of ripe tomatoes? The result is the increasingly known Tomatina in Buñol. This festival takes place in August and the streets of Buñol become a real battle of food, more specifically, of tomatoes.

A big and well-known battle all over the world, where participants throw tomatoes at each other. This friendly 'war' turns the streets into a red and juicy ocean for a whole hour.

WHAT DO THEY HAVE IN COMMON?

THEY BRING COMMUNITIES TOGETHER: They all gather people for public, family, or local events.

THEY HAVE RECOGNIZABLE SYMBOLS: Skulls and offerings for the Day of the Dead; national symbols for Australia Day and Canada Day; and red bandanas, the "Chupinazo" and bulls for the San Fermín festival.

THEY REINFORCE CULTURAL IDENTITY: The Day of the Dead keeps an indigenous and catholic tradition alive; Australia Day and Canada Day celebrate national identities; San Fermín expresses the identity and religious character of Pamplona.

MAIN DIFFERENCE

The Day of the Dead focuses on remembrance and respect fot the deceased; Australia Day and Canada Day are national holidays; and San Fermín is a religious festival.
